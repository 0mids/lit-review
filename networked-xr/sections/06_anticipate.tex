\section{Delivering Ahead of the User: Adaptation, Prediction and Caching}\label{sec:anticipate}

An immersive client displays only a fraction of what it could receive, and it must request that fraction before the user looks there. This section asks how a system decides what to fetch, at what quality and from where, ahead of the user's head and body (\cref{fig:anticipate-arch}). The function covers \fnAnticipateN{} papers (\fnAnticipateSh{} of the corpus), and its share fell from \fnAnticipateEarly{} in 2017--2019 to \fnAnticipateLate{} in 2022--2024. Rate adaptation and adaptive streaming remains the largest problem cell in the atlas, with \nxrNProbRateAdaptationStreaming{} papers, but it grew by \nxrCagrProbRateAdaptationStreaming{} a year while the corpus grew by \nxrCagrTotal{}. Viewport and motion prediction (\nxrNProbViewportMotionPrediction{} papers) and caching and prefetching (\nxrNProbCachingPrefetching{}) are smaller and growing faster. The decline tracks that of 360-degree video, the content all three were built for, whose share fell from \nxrShContVideoThreeSixtyEarly{} to \nxrShContVideoThreeSixtyLate{}. Evidence thins along the chain: \nxrRealRateAdaptationStreaming{} of rate-adaptation papers use a real testbed and \nxrUsersRateAdaptationStreaming{} involve users; prediction is judged mainly on recorded traces (\nxrTraceViewportMotionPrediction{}); caching is judged mainly in simulation (\nxrSimCachingPrefetching{}) and never with users. Codecs are treated in \cref{sec:encode}, remote rendering in \cref{sec:compute}.

\subsection{Viewport-adaptive streaming: spending bits where the user looks}

Tiled delivery of 360-degree video saves bandwidth, and the saving shrinks as the test becomes realistic: from up to 72\% with a scripted viewport to 26--35\% for Flare on phones over commercial LTE (\cref{tab:anticipate-savings}). The saving is paid for twice. Tiling costs coding efficiency, a median 7.3--10.4\% for 1-s tiles \cite{W2896840651} and up to 37.3\% for an 8$\times$5 grid \cite{W2623784065}; decoding many tiles raised Flare's phone CPU load from 18\% to 58\% \cite{W2896840651}. Prediction error is the main limiter, so controllers hedge: 360ProbDASH fetched tiles in proportion to their viewing probability and raised viewport PSNR by about 39\% \cite{W2766324256}.

How the controller should be built is contested. Deep reinforcement learning, used by about a quarter of the cell, reports 20--30\% gains over heuristics \cite{W2920378308,W3159074940}; BOLA360, a buffer-based controller with a near-optimality proof and no training, reports 6--110\% \cite{W4394890851}. The two families have never been compared on shared traces, and learned policies degrade on unseen viewing patterns and networks \cite{W4403826584,W4400409717}. Both optimise an assumed objective, a hand-weighted sum of viewport quality, rebuffering and switches. No abstract in the cell names an ITU-T QoE model, although tile extensions of P.1203 and P.1204.3 exist \cite{W3200608537,W3168206114}, and a database of 378 viewport videos found that mainstream quality models fail for viewport-adaptive streams \cite{W4402916896}.

Most of this evidence comes from one on-demand client on an emulated link. Live streaming removes the pre-encoded ladder: YouTube Live delivered 360-degree streams with 4.2--44.2~s camera-to-display delay depending on mode \cite{W4361857501}. Multicast exploits similar head motion across viewers and saved over 50\% \cite{W2725312916}, but adaptation for several headsets behind one bottleneck has reached users in no study. Wireless access is the largest blind spot: only two abstracts in the cell use commercial cellular traces \cite{W3021314748,W2810704880}, although mmWave variability already demands new mechanisms such as band switching \cite{W3195004621}.

\subsection{Six degrees of freedom: adapting volumetric scenes}

Volumetric streaming carries the idea into 6DoF, where the unit of adaptation becomes a 3D cell, an object or a level of detail, and visibility depends on position, distance and occlusion as well as direction. It is the fastest-growing part of rate adaptation. ViVo combined the three visibility tests and saved 40\% of data on average, and up to 80\%, over commercial 5G at SSIM above 0.99 \cite{W3021314748}; the same content saved 40\% for headset users but only 10\% for phone users, so the gain depends on how people move. No general allocation policy has emerged. PCC-DASH found that the best multi-object strategy depends on the scene and the camera path \cite{W2982262421}. The field assumes its navigation traces are representative, yet the newest public set has 15 participants \cite{W4408870156}, and about one volumetric adaptation paper in thirty evaluates with users.

Two-way volumetric communication breaks the buffer-based model altogether. Systems reuse low-latency DASH \cite{W3030878847} or WebRTC \cite{W4394890952}; a tiled user-adaptive point-cloud system needed 14~Mbps instead of about 300~Mbps in a 33-user study \cite{W4304091566}. Adaptation in these systems is coupled loosely, if at all, to congestion control, and layered Gaussian-splatting representations are judged on visual fidelity rather than on behaviour under variable throughput \cite{W4402704690}.

\subsection{Prediction: how far ahead, and against which baseline}

The prediction horizon couples the two halves of the controller (\cref{fig:anticipate-arch}). A deep buffer absorbs throughput dips but forces the system to predict seconds ahead; a shallow buffer needs only short predictions but stalls when throughput drops. Accuracy falls steeply with horizon: Flare's best predictor reached median accuracies of 90.5\%, 72.9\%, 58.2\% and 35.2\% at 0.2, 0.5, 1 and 3~s \cite{W2896840651}, and a cross-dataset benchmark found no algorithm accurate much beyond 300~ms \cite{W3089527252}. For on-demand content, earlier viewers are the strongest long-horizon signal: cross-user prediction raised accuracy by 20.2 percentage points \cite{W2896449077}. Live content has no such history and relies on online learning or object semantics \cite{W4243538177,W3141438256}.

Whether deep models beat simple predictors is the central controversy. On the Tsinghua dataset, persistence (keep the current viewport) reached a hit rate of 0.8355, against 0.8464 for an LSTM \cite{W2963428739}; for 6DoF pose, simple statistical models performed comparably well \cite{W4379805466}. Learned models report large gains mostly over other learned models. A fusion transformer reached an average precision of 0.954 at 1~s against 0.901 for the previous best, but on a random split that placed the same videos and users in training and test \cite{W4387148495}. Fewer than one prediction paper in ten names a persistence, linear or Kalman baseline.

Two further assumptions go untested. Controllers need calibrated uncertainty to size tile margins, yet predictors output point estimates; predicting the predictor's own deviation saved 45\% of bandwidth at under 0.1\% failure \cite{W2585813514}, but no paper the atlas read reports calibration. Accuracy is also assumed to translate into experience, yet closed-loop tests with real systems fell from 9 of 74 prediction papers in 2016--2022 to 1 of 54 in 2023--2026. Pose prediction that hides motion-to-photon (MTP) latency is discussed in \cref{sec:compute}.

\subsection{Caching and prefetching: placing content before it is requested}

Viewport-aware caching beats popularity-only replacement, a result replicated by independent groups but always against weak baselines. Placing tiles by cross-user viewport similarity raised the edge hit ratio by at least 40\% over LFU and 17\% over LRU \cite{W2896595273}. OpCASH covered more than 95\% of the viewport from cache after 24 views and fetched 85\% less data from the origin \cite{W4288370107}. Prefetching extends to AR: world-scale AR retrieval missed 20\% fewer deadlines with ten times fewer wasted bytes \cite{W4379932440}. Joint designs that also transcode or render at the edge report lower latency in simulation \cite{W2979447669,W4210795263}, a result that holds almost by construction, because the joint problem contains the decoupled one.

Three questions remain open. The first is granularity: fixed tiles, variable tiles that rarely recur \cite{W4288370107}, or similarity caching over continuous orientations \cite{W3156984197} have never been compared head to head. The second is the interaction with adaptation: cache hits distort the throughput estimates that DASH clients rely on \cite{W3122351391}, yet most caching papers assume an ideal client. The third is whether learned cache policies add anything beyond good viewport prediction, which cannot be settled while XRFlux remains the only shared benchmark \cite{W4409763640}. Beneath all three lies the assumption that hit ratio stands for experience. No caching scheme in the atlas has been evaluated with users, and fewer than one caching paper in twenty addresses 6DoF content, which is reusable across viewpoints and users.

\begin{openproblems}
\item \textbf{Prediction and adaptation are not validated in closed loop with users.} Real-system tests of predictors fell to 1 of 54 papers after 2022, and ABR objectives are hand-weighted. A tiled player that runs persistence, Kalman and learned predictors under ABR policies over real bandwidth, with a within-subjects rating study, would show whether accuracy rankings survive.
\item \textbf{No dataset joins head motion, network conditions and ratings from the same session.} Four dataset papers pair head data with network traces and four with ratings; replay removes the viewer's reaction to quality. Live sessions over Wi-Fi and commercial 5G, logging pose, packets and continuous ratings, would close this gap.
\item \textbf{Adaptation over real wireless access is under-evaluated.} The cell has half the expected 5G papers and two abstracts with commercial cellular traces. Paired head-motion and radio traces from walking and seated users, replayed in emulation and run live, would test whether PHY and MAC hints reduce stalls.
\item \textbf{6DoF adaptation and caching lack user-validated QoE and realistic navigation traces.} Volumetric streaming grows fastest, yet trace sets have about 15 participants and volumetric caching is almost unstudied. Larger 6DoF traces, paired comparisons of rendered views and a level-of-detail edge cache shared by co-located users are needed.
\item \textbf{Caching gains have never been shown to users or outside simulation.} No caching paper has users, and none of the coded, D2D or UAV caching papers uses traces or a testbed. An unmodified HTTP edge cache serving tiled DASH, comparing LRU, viewport-aware and Belady policies with viewers, would quantify perceptible benefit.
\end{openproblems}
